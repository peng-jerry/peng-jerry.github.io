Lightweight Electroadhesive Exo Tendon (LEET) System

ESTAT Actuation

April - August 2024

Skills: CAD(Solidworks), DFM, DFA, Tolerance Analysis, Rapid Prototyping

An important project I worked on while at ESTAT Actuation was the Lightweight Electroadhesive Exo-Tendon (LEET) System. The goal of the project was to design an exotendon-based support system to counterbalance helmet-induced loads and reduce chronic neck injury. The system would integrate seamlessly with the existing flight equipment while preserving comfort and safety in various flight conditions.

My primary responsibilities were designing components to ensure manufacturability, providing proper power distribution to all electrical components, and ensuring overall housing assemblability.

A big challenge for our design was ensuring that our electrical circuitry was able to pass Electrostatic Discharge Testing (ESD), (ensuring that our device doesn’t create sparks and catch on fire), and not losing power even in rough flight conditions.

A big innovation I made was to use pogo pins to help provide power. These are spring loaded connectors that provide consistent pressure and good contact quality, while allowing room for vertical error. In comparison to wires, they were much better at dealing with turbulence as well as eliminating the risk of generating a spark. This proved vital in creating a design that was both compact and reliable.

Overall, the LEET was one of the most complex systems I have ever worked on. It helped me to gain an appreciation of what it means to understand every little detail when designing. I developed a much better understanding of manufacturing techniques, the importance of constant design iteration and how to utilize rapid prototyping to produce a good product. This project not only improved my technical skills but also taught me how to think more critically, adapt to challenges, and refine ideas through repeated testing and feedback.
